Just because a choice feels confusing doesn't mean you don't know the answer.
You keep calling it a hard decision.
You sit with it. You turn it over.
You ask everyone you trust what they'd do.
But watch what you do with their answers.

You keep the ones that let you stay.
You argue with the ones that ask you to leave.
You ask again, hoping the answer changes.
You already know.
You knew on a Tuesday, in the car, in the quiet.
You knew when your chest tightened at one more year.


But this isn't a hard decision. It's a painful one.
Hard means you can't see it.
Painful means you can, and you don't want to.

Confusion is softer than grief.
So you stay confused, because confused still holds hope.
You're not deciding. You're negotiating.
That's not thinking. That's mourning early.
You can't see it through what you wanted it to be.


So stop asking what's right. Ask what's true, then go.
