There's a psychology rule about why correction feels personal.

When accountability is new, feedback feels like an attack.
If nobody ever coached you, only criticised you,
your body learned one lesson.
Correction means pain is coming.
So you defend before you even understand.

Someone names a mistake and your chest tightens.
Someone sets a boundary and you hear rejection.
Someone explains their side and you're already arguing back.


But being corrected is not being condemned.
One is about the thing you did.
The other is about who you are.
Most people are only talking about the thing.

When the sting comes, don't answer yet.
Breathe and count to five.
Then ask yourself one question. Is this true?
Keep the part that's true.
Drop the rest without a fight.

And learn the difference between explaining and excusing.
An explanation ends with what you'll do next.
An excuse ends with why you can't.
You can't control how they hear it.
You can only control whether you grow.


Wisdom has been chasing you. Let it catch up.
