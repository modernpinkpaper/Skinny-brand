There's a psychology idea called the helper's grip.
It says some help isn't kindness.
It's control wearing a kinder face.

You reminded them about the deadline.
You paid the bill they forgot.
You fixed the mess before anyone noticed.

And it felt like love.
But notice what it did for you.
Your chest got quiet.
The fear went away for an hour.

That's the tell.
Real help costs you something and leaves them stronger.
Control feels good to you and leaves them smaller.


But here's the part nobody says.
You can't be the glue for something that keeps choosing to break.
That's not love.
That's fear with your sleeves rolled up.

So try this.
Before you step in, ask one question.
Whose anxiety am I solving?

If it's yours, wait.
Let them feel the weight.
Offer help once, then let the answer be theirs.

Their irresponsibility is not your job.
Your boundary is not cruelty.
It's respect.


Love them enough to let them carry their own life.
