There's an old gardening rule that says
if a flower doesn't bloom,
you change the soil, not the flower.

Nobody shames a seed for staying small
in dirt that had nothing to give.
But you do it to yourself every day.

You call yourself lazy in a job that drained you.
You call yourself too sensitive in a room that laughed at you.
You call yourself difficult around people who never once asked how you were.

So you try to shrink into the space you were given.
You water yourself with books and habits and promises.
And you still wake up tired.


But maybe you were never the broken part.
That's not a flaw. That's a signal.

The same water hardens an egg and softens a potato.
Place is not small. Place is everything.
If you're the smartest, kindest person in the room, find another room.
It doesn't matter what you bring to the wrong table.


Stop pruning yourself to fit the dark.
Go find better light.
