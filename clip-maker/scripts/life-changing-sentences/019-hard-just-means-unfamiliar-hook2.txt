There's a theory that says nothing is easy or hard.
There is only familiar and unfamiliar.
Your brain doesn't measure difficulty.
It measures how many times you've done it.

So when something feels heavy, notice what you tell yourself.
You say you're not built for this.
You say other people just get it.
But you're not reading skill. You're reading newness.

Think about driving, and how the first week felt.
Think about typing without looking at your hands.
Think about a language you now speak without thinking.
All of it was once unfamiliar. None of it stayed that way.


So here's the practical part.
Step one of being good is being bad.
Not a little bad. Honestly bad.
And the only way through is repetitions, not talent.

Give it twenty tries before you judge yourself.
Count the reps, not the results.
Bad reps still count. Confused reps still count.
Someone worse than you is doing it confidently right now.

Difficulty isn't a verdict. It's a stage.


So next time it feels hard, say unfamiliar instead, and do it again.
